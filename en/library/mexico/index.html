10 Books to Understand Mexico

Mexico is the United States' largest trading partner, the birthplace of one of humanity's great civilizations, and a country locked in a century-long struggle between democratic aspirations and entrenched power. These ten books — by journalists, historians, and political scientists — map that struggle from the Aztecs to the cartel wars.

2

Distant Neighbors: A Portrait of the Mexicans

Alan Riding

1985 · Vintage

The classic portrait of Mexico by the New York Times Mexico City bureau chief. Riding's account of Mexican politics, culture, and society — written at the height of PRI dominance — remains the essential starting point four decades on. Sharp, fair, and extraordinarily well-observed.

3

El Narco: Inside Mexico's Criminal Insurgency

Ioan Grillo

2011 · Bloomsbury Press

The definitive account of how Mexico's cartels evolved from smugglers into a paramilitary insurgency. Grillo, who has covered Mexico for twenty years, writes with street-level reporting and clear structural analysis — neither sensationalism nor euphemism.

Amazon.comAmazon.co.uk
4

Mañana Forever?: Mexico and the Mexicans

Jorge G. Castañeda

2012 · Vintage

Mexico's former foreign minister examines the cultural traits — individualism, distrust of institutions, aversion to conflict — that shape Mexican politics and society. A frank insider's account of why reform is so hard and what would need to change.

Amazon.comAmazon.co.uk
5

Mexico: Biography of Power

Enrique Krauze

1997 · HarperCollins

Leading Mexican historian Krauze narrates two centuries of Mexican history through the biographies of its key leaders — from independence priests to revolutionary caudillos. An unmatched portrait of how personality and power interact in Mexican politics.

Amazon.comAmazon.co.uk
6

Narconomics: How to Run a Drug Cartel

Tom Wainwright

2016 · PublicAffairs

The Economist's former Mexico bureau chief applies economic analysis to the drug trade. Wainwright shows how cartels function like franchises, why supply-side enforcement fails, and what alternative policies could work — rigorous and highly readable.

Amazon.comAmazon.co.uk
7

Blood Gun Money: How America Arms Gangs and Cartels

Ioan Grillo

2021 · Bloomsbury Publishing

Grillo's follow-up to El Narco traces the flow of U.S. firearms into Mexican cartel hands — from gun shows to straw purchases to Fast and Furious. A cross-border investigation that reframes the cartel crisis as an American problem as much as a Mexican one.

Amazon.comAmazon.co.uk
8

Open Veins of Latin America: Five Centuries of the Pillage of a Continent

Eduardo Galeano

1971 · Monthly Review Press

The canonical left-wing reading of Latin American history as five centuries of extraction — silver, rubber, oil — by colonial and neocolonial powers. Hugo Chávez famously gave a copy to Barack Obama. Essential for understanding how Mexico (and the region) frames its relationship with the North.

Amazon.comAmazon.co.uk
9

The Mexico Reader: History, Culture, Politics

Gilbert M. Joseph & Timothy J. Henderson (eds.)

2002 · Duke University Press

The most comprehensive single-volume anthology on Mexico — primary sources, journalism, and scholarship from the pre-Columbian era to the post-PRI transition. Ideal for readers who want depth across all topics rather than a single argument.

Amazon.comAmazon.co.uk
10

A Concise History of Mexico

Brian R. Hamnett

2006 · Cambridge University Press

Cambridge's authoritative single-volume history of Mexico, from the Olmecs to NAFTA and beyond. Hamnett balances political, economic, and social history with clarity and depth — the essential academic baseline before diving into more specialized works.
