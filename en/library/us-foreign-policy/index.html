Essential Books on U.S. Foreign Policy

American foreign policy since 1945 has shaped — and frequently destabilized — every region covered by the Iris library. These books examine it without illusions: the strategic interests, the covert operations, the unintended consequences, and the structural pressures that define how the world's most powerful state relates to everyone else.

3

Overthrow: America's Century of Regime Change from Hawaii to Iraq

Stephen Kinzer

2006 · Times Books

Stephen Kinzer documents fourteen U.S.-sponsored regime changes from 1893 to 2003 — Hawaii, Iran, Guatemala, Chile, Iraq. The pattern he finds is consistent: short-term strategic calculation, long-term blowback. The essential historical primer on American interventionism.

4

Blowback: The Costs and Consequences of American Empire

Chalmers Johnson

2000 · Metropolitan Books

CIA analyst turned critic Chalmers Johnson coined the term that defined post-9/11 security debates: the unintended consequences of covert U.S. operations abroad returning home as violence. Written in 2000, its predictive accuracy made it required reading after the Twin Towers fell.

Amazon.comAmazon.co.uk
5

Destined for War: Can America and China Escape Thucydides's Trap?

Graham Allison

2017 · Houghton Mifflin Harcourt

Harvard's Graham Allison examines the 'Thucydides Trap' — the structural tendency for rising powers to clash with established ones. Of 16 historical case studies, 12 ended in war. The China-U.S. framework this book introduced is now standard in foreign policy analysis.

Amazon.comAmazon.co.uk
6

The Clash of Civilizations and the Remaking of World Order

Samuel P. Huntington

1996 · Simon & Schuster

The most argued-about foreign policy book of the past three decades. Huntington predicted post-Cold War conflict would be driven by civilizational identity rather than ideology — prescient in some ways, reductive in others, but the unavoidable reference point for any serious discussion of global order.

Amazon.comAmazon.co.uk
7

The Limits of Power: The End of American Exceptionalism

Andrew Bacevich

2008 · Metropolitan Books

A conservative military historian and Vietnam veteran's indictment of American imperial overreach. Bacevich argues the crisis of U.S. foreign policy is rooted in domestic dysfunction — military, political, and economic — not external threats, and that only Americans can fix it.

Amazon.comAmazon.co.uk
8

Legacy of Ashes: The History of the CIA

Tim Weiner

2007 · Doubleday

National Book Award–winning history of the CIA from its 1947 founding through the Iraq War. Drawing on 50,000 declassified documents and hundreds of interviews, Weiner's account is a sustained institutional autopsy: lofty mission, chronic failure, enormous consequences.
